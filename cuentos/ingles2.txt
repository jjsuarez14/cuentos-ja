=== en-06 | 0 | mar | noche | en | The Elephant Who Shared the Water | Compartir | Cuento original en inglés

Far away, in a dry and sunny land, there lived a big grey elephant called Ellie. Ellie was the tallest animal on the plain, and she had a long trunk, wide ears and a very kind heart.

That summer, it did not rain for many, many days. The grass turned yellow, the trees lost their leaves, and the little rivers slowly disappeared. All the animals were hot and thirsty, and every morning they walked a long way looking for water, but they always came back with nothing.

One afternoon, Ellie was walking near the old hills when she heard a soft sound under her feet. Drip, drip, drip. She stopped, listened carefully, and began to dig with her strong feet and her long trunk. She dug and dug until, suddenly, cool clear water came up from the ground and filled a little pool.

Ellie drank and drank until she was not thirsty anymore. Then she looked at the pool and thought about all the other animals. The pool was small, and if everyone came, maybe there would not be enough water for her tomorrow. For a moment, she wanted to keep the secret.

But then she remembered the little zebra who was always thirsty, the old lion who walked so slowly, and the baby monkeys who cried in the hot sun. So she lifted her trunk to the sky and called as loudly as she could. The sound traveled across the whole plain, and one by one, the animals came.

First came the zebras, then the giraffes, then the monkeys, and finally the old lion. They were surprised and happy, and they took turns drinking from the pool. Nobody pushed and nobody fought, because Ellie stood next to the water and made sure that everyone got a little.

And something wonderful happened. The more they shared, the more the animals helped each other. The giraffes found sweet leaves at the top of the trees and shared them. The monkeys found fruit and shared it too. Even the old lion told stories in the evening, so that the little ones were not afraid of the dark.

A few days later, the rain finally came. The rivers filled up again, the grass turned green, and the flowers opened. But the animals never forgot what Ellie had done.

That night, under a big silver moon, all the animals slept close together near the little pool. Ellie closed her eyes, and her kind heart felt warm and full, because she knew that sharing had made the whole plain a happier place.

The end.

At the bottom of a green valley there was a small red train called Toby. Toby was not very big and not very fast, but he was cheerful, and every day he carried apples, bread and milk to the little town at the top of the hill.

One morning, the station master gave Toby a very special job. A school on the other side of the mountain needed books, pencils and toys for the children, and all the big trains were busy. Toby was very proud, so he whistled happily and started his journey.

The first part of the trip was easy. Toby rolled past the farms, crossed a small bridge over the river and waved to the cows in the fields. But then he arrived at the foot of the mountain, and the track went up, up, up, much higher than the little hill he climbed every day.

Toby looked at the steep track and felt a little scared. He was small, and the wagons were heavy. For a moment he stopped and thought that maybe he could not do it. But then he remembered the children waiting for their books and toys, and he decided that he could try.

So Toby began to climb, slowly and carefully. His wheels turned and turned, and his little engine puffed and puffed. Halfway up the mountain, he was very tired and he started to slow down. He wanted to stop, but instead he took a deep breath, counted his wheels, one, two, three, four, and kept going.

When he was almost at the top, a big old train coming down the other track saw him and smiled. The old train shouted that Toby was doing a great job and that the top was very near. Those kind words gave Toby new strength, and with one last push, he reached the top of the mountain.

From up there, he could see the whole valley, the river shining in the sun, and the little school on the other side. Going down was easy and fun, and soon Toby arrived at the school. The children ran out, cheered and clapped, and they opened the boxes full of books, pencils and toys.

That evening, Toby rolled back to his station, tired but very happy. The station master gave him a shiny new bell as a prize. Toby went into his warm shed, closed his windows, and fell asleep, dreaming of tall mountains that he was not afraid to climb anymore.

The end.

=== en-08 | 0 | mar | noche | en | The Cat Who Lived in the Lighthouse | Calma para dormir | Cuento original en inglés

On a rocky island in the middle of the sea there stood a tall white lighthouse, and inside the lighthouse lived a fluffy orange cat called Pumpkin. Pumpkin lived with an old lighthouse keeper who had a white beard, a warm blue sweater and a gentle voice.

Every day was the same, and Pumpkin liked it that way. In the morning, she watched the fishing boats leave the harbor. At lunchtime, she ate a little fish that the keeper gave her. In the afternoon, she slept in a patch of sunshine on the windowsill, and the seagulls flew around the tower, calling to each other over the waves.

But her favorite moment came every evening, when the sun went down. As the sky turned orange and then purple, the keeper climbed the long spiral staircase, one step at a time, and Pumpkin followed him all the way to the top. There, in a little round room made of glass, was the great lamp.

The keeper cleaned the glass carefully, checked the oil and then lit the lamp. Slowly, the big light began to turn, round and round, sending a long golden beam across the dark water. Far away, the sailors saw the light and knew that they were safe and that they were close to home.

Pumpkin loved to sit next to the lamp and watch the light go round. She could see the boats coming back to the harbor, one by one, with their little lanterns shining. She could see the stars appearing in the sky and the moon rising slowly above the sea.

One night, a thick fog came in and covered everything. The keeper rang the big bell, dong, dong, dong, so that the boats could hear the way home. Pumpkin stayed close to him, purring softly, and together they watched and waited until the very last boat was safe in the harbor.

When the fog went away, the keeper smiled and stroked Pumpkin's soft fur. Then they went downstairs to the little kitchen, where he drank a cup of hot cocoa and she drank a bowl of warm milk.

Pumpkin curled up in her basket near the fire. The waves whispered against the rocks, the light kept turning high above, and the whole sea was quiet and calm. Slowly, Pumpkin closed her eyes and fell asleep, safe and warm in the tall white lighthouse.

The end.

On a warm beach, under the soft sand, a little turtle called Shelly hatched from her egg. She pushed and wiggled until she came out into the moonlight, and then, like all the other baby turtles, she started walking towards the sea.

The other turtles ran into the water and began to swim at once, moving their little flippers and disappearing into the waves. But when Shelly reached the water, a big wave came, picked her up and rolled her back onto the sand. She tried again, and another wave rolled her back again.

Shelly sat on the sand, wet and tired, and she felt like crying. All her brothers and sisters were already swimming, and she was still on the beach. She thought that maybe swimming was not for her.

Just then, an old crab walked by. He had seen many baby turtles on that beach, and he knew a little secret. He told Shelly that the waves were not her enemies, and that she only needed to wait for the right moment. When the wave goes back, he said, that is the time to go.

So Shelly watched the sea very carefully. She saw a wave come in, and she waited. Then she saw the wave go back out, and she ran as fast as her little legs could carry her. This time, the water carried her gently away from the beach.

But swimming was still difficult. At first, she moved her flippers too fast and went round in circles. Then she moved them too slowly and began to sink. She was a little scared, but she did not give up. She practiced moving her flippers slowly and strongly, again and again, and little by little she started to glide through the water.

By the time the sun came up, Shelly was swimming beautifully. She saw colorful fish, soft seaweed and a friendly dolphin who swam next to her for a while. She was so proud of herself, because she had not given up, even when it was difficult.

When night came again, Shelly found a quiet place between two rocks where the water was calm. The moon shone on the sea, the waves rocked her gently up and down, and the little turtle who had learned to swim closed her eyes and slept, dreaming of all the oceans she would explore.

The end.

In a big forest full of tall trees, there lived a brown bear called Bruno. All summer long, Bruno played in the river, climbed the hills and ate sweet berries and golden honey until his tummy was round and full.

But one morning in autumn, Bruno noticed that the forest was changing. The leaves on the trees were turning red, yellow and orange, and they were falling slowly to the ground. The air was colder in the mornings, the days were shorter, and the birds were flying away to warmer places.

Bruno's mother told him that winter was coming, and that bears have a special way of spending the winter. They sleep through the cold months, in a warm and cozy den, and they wake up in the spring when the flowers come back. Bruno thought that was the strangest and most wonderful idea he had ever heard.

So that week, Bruno and his mother got ready. They ate lots of nuts and berries, so they would have enough energy for the long sleep. They found a cave under the roots of an old tree, and they filled it with soft leaves, dry grass and moss, until it was the coziest bed in the whole forest.

Before going to sleep, Bruno wanted to say goodbye to his friends. He visited the squirrel, who was busy hiding acorns, and the rabbit, who was making her burrow warmer. He waved to the owl, who would stay awake all winter, watching over the forest. They all wished him sweet dreams and promised to see him in the spring.

Then the first snow began to fall. It came down softly, in tiny white flakes that covered the trees, the hills and the river. Bruno watched it from the entrance of the cave, and he thought the forest had never looked so quiet and so beautiful.

His mother called him inside, and Bruno walked into the den and lay down next to her. The leaves were soft, the moss was warm, and his mother's fur smelled like honey and pine trees. Outside, the snow kept falling, gently covering the entrance like a white blanket.

Bruno yawned a very big yawn. His eyes felt heavy, his paws felt warm, and his breathing became slow and deep. And as the snow fell softly over the sleeping forest, the little bear closed his eyes and began his long, long winter sleep.

The end.

=== en-11 | 0 | jue | noche | en | The Owl and the Stormy Night | No tenerle miedo a la tormenta | Cuento original en inglés

High up in an old oak tree, in a round hole in the trunk, there lived a little owl called Ollie. Ollie had big yellow eyes, soft brown feathers and two little tufts on his head that looked like ears. Every night he flew around the forest with his father, learning to see in the dark and listening to the sounds of the night.

One evening, the sky became very dark, and the wind began to blow harder and harder. The trees started to move and the leaves flew in every direction. Then, suddenly, a bright light flashed across the sky, and a moment later there was a loud boom. Ollie was so scared that he hid deep inside the hole, covering his eyes with his wings.

His father came in and sat next to him. He told Ollie that it was only a storm, and that storms are noisy but they always pass. He explained that the light was lightning, the boom was thunder, and the water falling from the sky was rain that the forest needed to grow.

Then his father taught him a little trick. When he saw the light, they would count together slowly until they heard the thunder. If they counted more numbers each time, it meant that the storm was moving away. So they waited, and when the next light came, they counted: one, two, three. Boom. The next time, they counted to five. Boom. And the next time, they counted all the way to eight, and the boom was much quieter.

Ollie was not scared anymore. Now the storm was like a game. He peeked out of the hole and watched the rain falling on the leaves and dripping from the branches. He listened to the sound it made, soft and steady, like a song. He even saw a family of rabbits running to their burrow, and a fox curling up under a big rock, all of them safe and dry.

Little by little, the thunder stopped, the wind became calm, and the rain became a gentle drizzle. The clouds moved away, and the moon came out again, shining on the wet forest and making every drop of water sparkle like a tiny star.

Ollie snuggled under his father's wing. The forest smelled fresh and clean, and everything was quiet again. He closed his big yellow eyes and fell asleep, listening to the last soft drops of rain.

The end.

But Grandma Rose's garden had a secret. The flowers grew when people said kind words near them. When someone said "please", a little daisy opened. When someone said "thank you", a red tulip stood up tall. And when someone said "I'm sorry", a whole bush of roses burst into bloom.

Tom was so surprised that he laughed. Then he gave the ball back to the other children and said "please" when he wanted to play, and a row of yellow daisies opened at his feet.

By the end of the afternoon, the garden was more colorful than ever before. There were flowers everywhere, and all the children were playing together, laughing and taking turns. Tom felt warm and happy inside, and he thought that kind words were a kind of magic.

When the sun went down, the children went home for dinner. Tom said goodbye and thanked Grandma Rose for letting him play. As he walked away, one last little flower opened by the gate, glowing softly in the evening light.

That night, Tom fell asleep thinking about the garden, and in his dreams, every word he said turned into a flower.

First, the moon passes over the big city. She sees the tall buildings with their windows full of light, and one by one she watches the lights go out as families get ready for bed. She sees a baker closing his shop, a bus driving slowly down an empty street, and a cat walking along a wall, looking for a warm place to sleep.

Then the moon passes over the countryside. The fields are dark and still, and the farm animals are sleeping in the barn. After that, the moon passes over the forest. The trees are quiet, and their branches move gently in the wind. A family of deer is resting under an old tree, a fox is curled up in his den, and the little birds are hiding their heads under their wings. The moon shines softly between the leaves, so that nobody in the forest feels afraid of the dark.

Next, the moon reaches the sea. The waves are calm, and they make a soft sound as they touch the sand. The fishing boats are resting in the harbor, rocking up and down, and the lighthouse is turning its light round and round. The moon looks at her reflection in the water and smiles, because the sea looks like a mirror full of stars.

At last, the moon comes to a little house with a little window. Inside, in a cozy bed, there is a little child under a warm blanket, with a favorite toy close by. The moon looks through the window and sends a beam of soft silver light across the bedroom.

The child is sleepy, very sleepy. The moon watches over the whole world, the city, the farm, the forest and the sea, and now she watches over this little room too. Everything is quiet, everything is safe, and it is time to sleep.
